# Telos — A Plain-English Introduction

*For people who want to understand what Telos does without digging into the technical details. If you want the full technical picture, see `SESSION_ZERO.md`.*

---

## What is Telos?

Telos helps your company reach its goals **reliably**, even when the world is full of uncertainty.

You tell Telos what you want to achieve. Telos helps you break it into steps, decide who does what (people or AI), track progress, check whether each step worked as promised, and tell you what to improve.

**Think of Telos as an operating system for how your company works.**

---

## What makes Telos different?

Most tools do one piece of the puzzle:

- Jira tracks tasks
- OKR trackers track goals
- Grafana tracks metrics
- BPM tools run workflows

**Telos does the whole cycle.** It takes your goal, breaks it down, runs the work, measures progress, and recommends improvements — all in one place.

It has four unusual properties:

1. **It works with uncertainty.** When you say "I want to reduce customer churn by 5%," Telos does not pretend this is certain. It tracks the probability of each step succeeding and tells you when things drift.

2. **It treats AI agents as real participants.** AI is not just an assistant. As agents prove reliable, Telos lets them take on more responsibility — from doing a single task to running a whole campaign.

3. **It improves itself.** Telos watches which parts of your work are reliable and which are not, and recommends what to change.

4. **It uses methodologies you already know.** OKR, Impact Mapping, Kanban — pick one or combine several. Telos does not force a new way of thinking. It makes the ways you already know more reliable.

## What can I do with Telos?

### Example 1: Hire three engineers in a month

1. You tell Telos the goal: *"Hire 3 engineers within 30 days."*
2. Telos helps you break it into steps:
   - Find 100 candidates
   - Interview 20
   - Make 5 offers
   - Accept 3
3. Each step gets a **promise**: for example, *"We expect 10 good candidates out of 100 applications (±3)."*
4. Some steps an AI agent can do (outreach, scheduling). Some are for people (interviewing).
5. As the work happens, Telos checks each promise. If a step misses its promise, Telos tells you and suggests what to try next.
6. At the end, you do not just see "3 hired." You see which parts of the process were reliable and which to improve next time.

### Example 2: Reduce customer churn

1. You tell Telos: *"Reduce churn from 5% to 3% by the end of Q4."*
2. Using a methodology such as Impact Mapping, Telos helps you break it down:
   - **Who** is involved: customers starting to disengage; your support team
   - **What behavior change** you want: more logins; faster support responses
   - **How** you will influence that: nudge emails, check-in calls, faster ticket triage
3. These become parallel projects. Each has a promise. Some are done by AI, some by people.
4. Telos runs them in parallel. Some succeed. Some fail. Telos records everything.
5. At the end of Q4, Telos tells you: *"Churn dropped to 3.4%. The nudge emails worked. The check-in calls did not. The triage improvements mattered most. Here is what to try next quarter."*

### Example 3: Launch a new product

Same pattern. Tell Telos the goal. Choose a methodology (Jobs-to-be-Done combined with Kanban works well for products). Assign actors. Run the cycle. Get back not just "launched" but a readout of what worked and what did not.

## Who should use Telos?

Anyone with a goal and a team trying to reach it. This includes:

- **Small business owners** tracking growth
- **Product teams** building features
- **Marketing teams** running campaigns
- **Operations teams** managing workflows
- **Founders** tracking company-wide goals
- **Enterprise teams** running large initiatives

The same system scales from a 10-person startup to a 1000-person company. It works because the underlying idea — "everything is a process with a promise and evidence" — applies at every size.

---

## Do I need to be technical to use Telos?

No. You declare your goal in plain language. Telos and its AI agents handle the rest. You review, approve, and take over wherever you want.

The only things you need to understand are:

- **What you want to achieve** (the goal)
- **Who does the work** (people, AI, or both)
- **Whether the promises held** (the feedback Telos gives you)

---

## How does Telos work, in plain words?

1. **You declare a goal.** Not "grow the business." Something concrete: *"Retention ≥ 80% by Q4, with 70% confidence."* Numbers and confidence.

2. **Telos breaks it down.** Using a methodology you choose (or a combination), Telos turns your goal into a tree of smaller goals.

3. **Actors are assigned.** Each small goal has an owner — a person, an AI agent, a team, or an external system.

4. **Each piece of work has a promise.** For example: *"This email campaign will raise engagement by 5% ±2 for 30% of our customers."* Not "we will try our best" — a number.

5. **The work runs in parallel.** Many things happen at once.

6. **Telos checks promises.** After each step, did the promise hold? If yes, the step is done. If no, Telos records the failure as a fact and may trigger an alternative.

7. **Telos improves itself.** Over time, you see which promises tend to hold, which do not, and where to change your approach.

---

## What should I know about AI agents in Telos?

Telos treats AI agents as participants, not just tools. Their responsibility grows as they prove reliable. There are four levels:

- **L0 — Tool.** You ask the agent to do one thing. You own the result. Example: asking ChatGPT to draft one email.

- **L1 — Human-led.** The agent helps. You decide. Example: coding with GitHub Copilot — every suggestion is yours to accept or reject.

- **L2 — Agent-led.** The agent runs a process. You approve key moments. Example: the agent runs a content pipeline and you approve each article before publication.

- **L3 — Autonomous.** The agent runs end-to-end. You step in only if something fails. Example: the agent manages nightly data jobs; a human gets notified only on failure.

Telos helps you move agents up this ladder **only when data justifies it**. If an agent has been 95% reliable at L2 for three months, it can earn L3. If it starts failing, it drops back down.

You stay in control. Trust is earned, not assumed.

## What does "good" look like with Telos?

A company using Telos well will have:

- **Every goal has numbers.** No vague "improve customer experience." Only "reduce ticket response time to 2 hours with 80% confidence by Q3."
- **Every piece of work has a promise.** People know exactly what they are committing to, and can raise a flag when the promise is at risk.
- **Failures are recorded as facts, not hidden.** Bad outcomes become learning input, not material for blame.
- **People and AI both contribute.** Each does what they are best at.
- **Everyone can see how their work connects to the big picture.** The goal tree makes it visible.
- **The team gets smarter over time.** Patterns of what works and what does not become explicit.

---

## What does Telos NOT do?

- **Telos does not replace your judgment.** You still decide what goals matter.
- **Telos does not guarantee success.** It makes your uncertainty visible and gives you tools to manage it, but the world is still uncertain.
- **Telos does not automate everything.** Many decisions still belong to people. AI agents earn more responsibility over time, but only with data to back it up.
- **Telos does not force a specific methodology.** Pick what works for you. Change it when it stops working.

## Quick glossary — plain-language version

- **Goal** — what you want to achieve, with a number and a confidence level.
- **Process** — anything that changes the state of your company (a campaign, a hire, a metric collection, a meeting).
- **Actor** — a person, AI agent, team, or external system that does the work and is accountable for it.
- **Tool** — something used during the work but not responsible for the outcome. A spreadsheet is a tool. An AI agent asked for one draft is a tool.
- **Promise / Contract** — what a process commits to deliver, expressed in numbers with tolerances.
- **Evidence** — how we know whether the promise held. Can be a test result, a log, a metric, or a sign-off.
- **Methodology** — a way of breaking goals into work. Telos supports several — OKR, Impact Mapping, Kanban, Jobs-to-be-Done, and more. Pick what fits your situation.
